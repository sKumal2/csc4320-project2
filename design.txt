Section 3: The shared FIFO queue

Concept: You have to build your own bounded queue; you can't use a library blocking queue. The usual design is a circular buffer: a fixed-size array plus three numbers.
- head: where the next item will be taken from
- tail: where the next item will be put
- count: how many items are in the queue right now

When tail reaches the end of the array, it wraps back to 0. Work out the % arithmetic for that yourself.

Your text should answer:
- What data structure, and what fields?
- What do put and get do, step by step?
- What does one record hold: a job ID, and how do you represent STOP?
- How does this design guarantee FIFO order?

Section 4: Synchronization (one subsection per mode)

Mutex: Exactly one thread at a time may touch head, tail, or count. Say which data the mutex protects and what stays outside it. Processing a job and any delay should happen after unlocking; otherwise consumers can't run in parallel.

CV mode, concept: use two condition variables.
- not_empty: consumers wait on this while count == 0
- not_full: producers wait on this while count == capacity

Two rules you need to be able to explain:
1. Wait inside a while loop, not an if. A thread can wake up and find the condition is still false, either because of a spurious wakeup or because another thread got there first.
2. After a put, notify not_empty. After a get, notify not_full.

Semaphore mode, concept: use three primitives.
- empty starts at capacity and counts free slots.
- full starts at 0 and counts filled slots.
- A mutex protects the buffer.

A producer does empty.acquire(), then locks, puts, unlocks, then full.release(). A consumer does the mirror image.

A question to think through: what happens if a producer locks the mutex first and then calls empty.acquire() while the queue is full? Trace it. That deadlock is a classic demo question.

Race mode: in one or two sentences, how will you force two threads to read shared data before either one writes it? Your plan mentions gates or a barrier.

Section 5: Shutdown

Concept: You can't just have consumers quit when the queue is empty, because producers might still be about to add jobs. The standard fix is a sentinel, also called a "poison pill": a special STOP record.

Your text should answer:
- Why does main insert the STOPs only after joining all producers? Hint: FIFO order then guarantees every real job sits ahead of every STOP.
- Why exactly one STOP per consumer? What breaks with fewer? With more?
- What does a consumer do when it gets a STOP? (It logs STOP_GET and exits its loop.)
- After that, main joins the consumers and prints the summary.
